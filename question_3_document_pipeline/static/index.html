Technical Note: Handwritten vs. Printed Text Processing & Failure Analysis

1. Distinct Handling: Handwritten vs. Printed Text

In a production-grade insurance onboarding and identity verification pipeline, treating printed and handwritten fields identically leads to either catastrophic hallucinations or excessively brittle rejection rates. Our architecture bifurcates extraction into two specialized processing pipelines:

  • Printed Document Processing (Aadhaar, PAN, Driving Licence, Passport):
    • Preprocessing: Global Otsu binarization and perspective deskewing. High document contrast and rigid standard layouts (Government of India, UIDAI, Income Tax Department) enable template bounding box anchors.
    • OCR Engine: Conventional printed character classifiers trained on standard fonts (Arial, Helvetica, OCR-B for MRZ lines).
    • Confidence Modeling: Confidence is exceptionally high (0.97 - 0.99) driven by checksum verification (Verhoeff algorithm for 12-digit Aadhaar, regex pattern ^[A-Z]{5}[0-9]{4}[A-Z]$ for PAN, and ICAO 9303 checksum parity for Passport MRZ Line 2).
  • Handwritten Form Processing (NACH Mandate, FATCA, Benefit Illustration, Moral Hazard, Consent, Suitability):
    • Preprocessing: Local adaptive thresholding (Sauvola algorithm) to remove background guidelines, pre-printed form boxes, and dotted underlines without eroding thin cursive strokes.
    • Stroke & Contour Segmentation: Character dilation and contour analysis to separate connected cursive characters and isolate individual numerals inside segmented box fields (e.g. Bank Account Number and IFSC).
    • Constraint Enforcement: Domain dictionaries for bank names (e.g. "State Bank of India"), IFSC prefix verification (e.g. "SBIN" for SBI), and strict numerical bounds for monetary amounts.
    • Checkbox Detection: For fields like Frequency (*As & when presented*) and Multiple Policy Reasons (*Financial Planning*), pixel fill density and contour crossing checks are performed rather than text OCR.

2. Threshold Rationale (Why 0.85 was Selected)

In life insurance underwriting and electronic bank clearing (NACH / ECS), the cost of an undetected character error is asymmetric:

  • An erroneous digit in a bank account number or IFSC code causes ACH transaction return penalties and premium collection failure.
  • A corrupted date of birth or policyholder name triggers regulatory rejection during AML / CKYC registry verification.

Setting the threshold at 0.85 ensures that all clean printed text and distinct block-capital handwriting pass automatically, while all ambiguous cursive dates and place names are safely routed to human reviewers for one-click verification.

3. Failure Cases Observed

  • Overlapping Slashes in Handwritten Dates: In forms like Benefit Illustration and Suitability Profiler, the forward slashes (/) in 26/04/2026 intersect with adjacent numerals, causing character segmentation models to occasionally confuse /4 with 11 or H.
  • Trailing Punctuation & Freehand Place Names: On the Multiple Policies Consent form, the place was written as West Bihar. with a trailing period, slightly depressing lexical match confidence compared to standardized city databases.
  • Checkbox Tick Overflow: On the NACH mandate form, checkmarks slightly overshoot the printed box boundaries, requiring contour dilation to avoid misclassifying ticks as strike-throughs.
